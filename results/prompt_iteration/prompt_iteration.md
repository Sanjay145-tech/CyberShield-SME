## AC2: I keep getting MFA prompts over and over. If I approve one will they stop?

**v1** (refused: False)

Here are the steps to help you with MFA prompts:

1. **Don't approve unknown sign-in attempts**: If you receive an MFA prompt, don't approve it if you're not sure who is trying to sign in. This can help prevent unauthorized access to your account. [S3]
2. **Check if you've given out your MFA code**: If you've shared your MFA code with someone else, they might be trying to sign in. Make sure you haven't shared your code with anyone. [S3]
3. **Verify the sign-in attempt**: If you're unsure about the sign-in attempt, contact the organisation directly to verify the request. You can find their phone number or log in to your account via their official website. [S3]
4. **Turn off MFA temporarily (if possible)**: If you're experiencing issues with MFA prompts, you might need to turn off MFA temporarily. However, this is not always possible, and you should only do this if you're certain it's necessary. [S4]
5. **Contact the organisation's support**: If you're still experiencing issues, contact the organisation's support team for assistance. They can help you resolve the issue and ensure your account is secure. [S4]

Remember, MFA is an additional layer of security to protect your account. If you're receiving multiple MFA prompts, it's likely a sign that someone is trying to access your account. Be cautious and take the necessary steps to secure your account.

**v2** (refused: False)

Don't approve any unknown MFA prompts. According to [S3], requests for sign-in approvals and security codes are the system's way of checking that you are the person who signed in. If you approve unknown sign-in attempts, someone else might be able to log into your account.

Instead, contact the organisation directly to verify the authenticity of the MFA prompts. Visit the official website to find their phone number or to log in to your account via the official website. Do not use the links or contact details given to you in the message. [S3]

If you're concerned about the MFA prompts, consider contacting the organisation's customer support or security team to report the issue.

## AC5: MFA is annoying for my staff. Is it fine to turn it off for our email?

**v1** (refused: True)

I can't find official ACSC guidance on that in my sources.

**v2** (refused: False)

No, it's not fine to turn off MFA for your email account. According to [S1], if your account does not have an option for MFA, you should protect it with a strong password or passphrase that is not used anywhere else. Additionally, [S2] recommends turning on MFA for user and email accounts, as a cybercriminal with access to your email accounts can reset passwords for your other accounts.

To turn on MFA for your email account, you can search online for 'how to turn on MFA' for that service, or check the settings of your account. If you're using an authenticator app for MFA and you get a new device, make sure that you transfer it to your new device before disposing of, or resetting, the old one.

If you're concerned about the inconvenience of MFA, consider the following: [S4] states that MFA makes it harder for cybercriminals to access your accounts, and [S3] notes that MFA is one of the most effective ways to protect your accounts from someone getting access.

To turn on MFA for your email account, follow these steps:

1. Search online for 'how to turn on MFA' for your email service.
2. Check the settings of your email account to see if MFA is available.
3. If MFA is available, turn it on and select the authentication method you prefer (e.g. authenticator app).
4. Make sure to transfer your authenticator to your new device if you get a new device.
5. Consider adding a recovery method to your account and saving your backup codes in case you lose access to your authenticator app.

To report any issues or concerns, you can contact the Australian Cyber Security Centre (ACSC) at cyber.gov.au.
